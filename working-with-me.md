# Working with me 🤝

I care a lot about the experience of doing good work.

I like teams that are scrappy, pragmatic, and capable of getting shit done. I also think there's a huge difference between **moving quickly** and **making people miserable**.

Here are a few things that tend to make working with me easier.

## I ask a lot of questions

If you bring me a problem, expect me to ask why.

Not because I don't want to do the work. Usually the opposite: understanding the underlying goal gives us more options for solving it.

I also tend to think about who's *not* in the room. Technical decisions can have product, operational, organizational, or customer consequences that aren't immediately obvious from the code.

## I over-communicate

I'd rather give someone useful context than leave them guessing.

That doesn't mean I like walls of text. I care a lot about making complicated things easy to consume by thinking through:

- What do you need to know right now?
- What's useful context?
- What can wait?
- What decision are we actually making?

I like drafting, iterating, writing things down, and making decisions discoverable later.

## Strong opinions, loosely held

I will probably have an opinion.

I will also probably be willing to change it.

If you disagree with me, tell me why. You may have context I don't. Understanding that context is usually more valuable than winning the argument.

I don't expect consensus for its own sake.

## I like making decisions reversible

When something is low-risk or reversible, I don't particularly want us to spend three weeks making it feel permanent.

One pattern I use a lot is:

> **"This is what I propose. Does anyone object to me going ahead?"**

The question isn't whether everyone thinks it's perfect, it's whether anyone thinks it's **unsafe to try**.

That tends to keep things moving while still giving people a chance to surface concerns.

And when we've made a decision, I'm comfortable with:

> **Disagree and commit.**

## I like teaching

Teaching and mentoring are some of my favourite parts of engineering.

I care less about giving someone the answer than helping them develop the mental model that lets them find the answer themselves next time.

If we're working together and I think there's something you could learn from a problem we're solving, I may spend a little extra time explaining the *why*.

That's usually intentional.

## Feedback

I prefer feedback that's:

- timely
- specific
- actionable

Tell me what happened. Give me an example. Tell me what you think I could do differently.

I'll do the same for you.

I don't particularly value feedback that's vague enough to be impossible to act on, or feedback that arrives six months after the thing happened.

## The important bit

I want us to do good work **and** have a good experience doing it.

I'm not interested in 200-hour work weeks, hero culture, or treating AI as a substitute for thinking.

Being ambitious about the work and being thoughtful about the humans doing it aren't opposing ideas.
