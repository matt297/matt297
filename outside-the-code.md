# Outside the code 🏺

I spend a lot of my working life sitting in front of a computer, so most of my hobbies are things that give my brain a different kind of problem to solve.

I also tend to be a bit of a tinkerer. My hobbies come in seasons — I'll make a lot of pottery for a while, then disappear into cooking, then get obsessed with something else entirely.

## 🏺 Pottery

I started taking pottery classes in 2022 after years of saying I should try it.

I grew up in a home full of handmade ceramics, so there's something particularly satisfying about eventually learning to make them myself.

Part of what I love is that pottery produces something **physical**.

I spend my days making things that exist inside computers. Pottery gives me something I can actually hold, eat from, or drink out of.

It's also a very different kind of feedback loop.

With software, you can introduce a bug and potentially fix it five minutes later. With pottery, you can spend hours making something, wait for it to dry, wait for it to be fired, glaze it, fire it again, and only then discover what actually happened.

That forces a different kind of patience and experimentation.

I sell some of my pieces at cost, mostly so I can make room for more experiments. I intentionally don't treat pottery as a business.

There's value in having things in your life that are allowed to exist purely because they're fun.

My aunt, who is also a potter, once described handmade ceramics as being like choosing who you have coffee with every morning: when your cupboard is full of things made by different people, every mug carries a little reminder that another human being made this thing.

I like that.

## 🍳 Cooking & baking

I love cooking and baking, and I have a particularly enthusiastic relationship with the [New York Times Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/) app.

My general approach to recipes is:

**Make it as written → figure out what I think → change it next time.**

I like starting from the assumption that someone who has spent years developing a recipe probably knows something I don't.

One of my all-time favourites is Martha Stewart's [banana bread](https://www.marthastewart.com/312772/banana-bread).

## 🧋 Bubble tea

My go-to order is **jasmine milk tea with pearls** (50% sugar, in case you're curious).

When I say I like bubble tea, I mean the employees at my local shop know my name and order and have occasionally started making my drink before I've reached the counter.

I can also name every bubble tea shop within a kilometre of my home and tell you which one is best for basically every category of drink.

If bubble tea sommelier were a real profession, I'd be qualified.

I have, on occasion, treated bubble tea as a meal.

I'm aware this is nutritionally questionable.

I maintain that happiness is a nutrient.

## 🏙️ Urban design

I spent a semester in Copenhagen during university and took a course on Danish architecture and urban design.

It was one of the first times I encountered the idea that architecture isn't just about what a building looks like — **the design of a space can actively influence how people live within it.**

I remember learning about Bjarke Ingels' design of [Tietgenkollegiet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tietgen_Residence_Hall), where the kitchens face inward toward a shared courtyard.

During Copenhagen's dark winters, residents can look across the courtyard and see other people going about their lives.

It's a small design decision, but it changes the experience of living there.

That's the kind of design I find interesting: not just *does this work?*, but **what kind of experience does this create within the larger system it's part of?**

Apparently I think about software this way too.
